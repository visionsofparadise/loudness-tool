# Transcription of Recommendation ITU-R BS.1770-5 (11/2023)

This file is a transcription of ITU-R Recommendation BS.1770-5 (November 2023), *Algorithms to measure audio programme loudness and true-peak audio level*, for offline use. Source: official English PDF from https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770 (document `R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf`). Figures are omitted; every normative constant, table, and formula needed to implement K-weighted gated loudness and Annex-2 true-peak measurement is transcribed.

LKFS in the Recommendation is the same unit as LUFS.

## Scope

The Recommendation specifies audio measurement algorithms for determining subjective programme loudness and true-peak signal level.

## Recommends (measurement choice)

1. For loudness of an audio channel or programme with up to five main channels per ITU-R BS.775 (mono, stereo, and 3/2 multichannel), use the algorithm in Annex 1.
2. For a larger number of channels (ITU-R BS.2051 configurations), use Annex 3.
3. For object-based audio, or a mix of channel- and object-based audio, use Annex 4.
4. Programme-production loudness indication may be based on Annexes 1, 3, and 4.
5. True-peak indication of a digital audio signal should be based on the guidelines in Annex 2, or on a method that gives similar or superior results.

Users should be aware that measured loudness is an estimation of subjective loudness and involves some degree of uncertainty depending on listeners, audio material, and listening conditions. Compliance testing may use material from Report ITU-R BS.2217.

## Annex 1 — Objective multichannel loudness measurement

The algorithm has four stages:

1. "K" frequency weighting.
2. Mean-square calculation for each channel.
3. Channel-weighted summation (surround channels have larger weights; the LFE channel is excluded).
4. Gating of 400 ms blocks overlapping by 75%, using two thresholds:
   - the first at -70 LKFS;
   - the second at -10 dB relative to the level measured after application of the first threshold.

The block diagram admits five main channels (left, centre, right, left surround, right surround). Programmes with fewer than five channels leave unused inputs unused. The LFE channel is not included in the measurement.

### K-weighting (two-stage pre-filter)

The first step applies a two-stage pre-filter. The first stage accounts for the acoustic effects of the head, modelled as a rigid sphere. The second stage is a high-pass filter (revised low-frequency B-curve, RLB). Concatenation of the two stages is designated K-weighting.

Both stages are second-order filters of the form:

```
y[n] = b0*x[n] + b1*x[n-1] + b2*x[n-2] - a1*y[n-1] - a2*y[n-2]
```

These filter coefficients are for a sampling rate of 48 kHz. Implementations at other sampling rates require different coefficient values chosen to provide the same frequency response that the specified filter provides at 48 kHz. The values of these coefficients may need to be quantized due to internal precision; tests have shown that the algorithm is not sensitive to small variations.

#### Table 1 — Filter coefficients for stage 1 of the pre-filter (spherical-head model), 48 kHz

| ----------- | ---------------- |
| `b0`        | 1.53512485958697 |
| `b1`        | -2.69169618940638 |
| `b2`        | 1.19839281085285 |
| `a1`        | -1.69065929318241 |
| `a2`        | 0.73248077421585 |

(The implicit `a0` of this form is 1.)

### Mean square

The power, the mean square of the filtered input signal in a measurement interval `T`, is:

```
z_i = (1/T) * integral_0^T y_i(t)^2 dt                         (1)
```

where `y_i` is the input signal filtered by the two-stage pre-filter, and `i` belongs to `I = {L, R, C, Ls, Rs}`, the set of input channels.

### Ungated loudness

The loudness over the measurement interval `T` is:

```
Loudness, LK = -0.691 + 10 * log10( sum_i G_i * z_i )          LKFS  (2)
```

where `G_i` are the weighting coefficients for the individual channels.

The numerical result of equation (2) is followed by the designation LKFS: Loudness, K-weighted, relative to nominal full scale. The LKFS unit is equivalent to a decibel: an increase in the level of a signal by 1 dB causes the loudness reading to increase by 1 LKFS.

If a 0 dB FS, 1 kHz (997 Hz to be exact) sine wave is applied to the left, centre, or right channel input, the indicated loudness equals -3.01 LKFS. The constant -0.691 in equation (2) cancels out the K-weighting gain for 997 Hz. IEC 61606 states that unless otherwise specified, the reference frequency for measurement shall be the actual frequency 997 Hz, which may be stated in non-critical contexts as the nominal frequency 1 kHz.

#### Table 3 — Weightings for the individual audio channels

| Channel                    | Weighting, `G_i`   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Left (`G_L`)               | 1.0 (0 dB)         |
| Right (`G_R`)              | 1.0 (0 dB)         |
| Centre (`G_C`)             | 1.0 (0 dB)         |
| Left surround (`G_Ls`)     | 1.41 (~ +1.5 dB)   |
| Right surround (`G_Rs`)    | 1.41 (~ +1.5 dB)   |

The algorithm is effective for typical broadcast programme material and is not, in general, suitable for estimating the subjective loudness of pure tones.

### Gating

To calculate a gated loudness measurement, the interval `T` is divided into a set of overlapping gating block intervals. A gating block is a set of contiguous audio samples of duration `T_g = 400 ms`, to the nearest sample. The overlap of each gating block shall be 75% of the gating block duration (step = 100 ms at 48 kHz: 19200 samples per block, 4800-sample hop).

The measurement interval shall be constrained such that it ends at the end of a gating block. Incomplete gating blocks at the end of the measurement interval are not used.

The power, the mean square of the `j`th gating block of the `i`th input channel in the interval `T`, is:

```
z_ij = (1/T_g) * integral_{T_g * j * step}^{T_g * (j * step + 1)} y_i(t)^2 dt     (3)
```

where `step = 1 - overlap`, and

```
j ∈ { 0, 1, 2, ..., floor( (T - T_g) / (T_g * step) ) }
```

Incomplete blocks past the end of `T` are discarded.

The `j`th gating block loudness is:

```
l_j = -0.691 + 10 * log10( sum_i G_i * z_ij )                                    (4)
```

For a gating threshold `Gamma` there is a set of gating block indices `J_g = { j : l_j > Gamma }` where the gating block loudness is above the gating threshold. The number of elements in `J_g` is `|J_g|`.

The gated loudness of the measurement interval `T` is:

```
Gated loudness, LK_G = -0.691 + 10 * log10( sum_i G_i * (1/|J_g|) * sum_{j in J_g} z_ij )   LKFS  (5)
```

That is: average the mean-square energy of the surviving blocks (the energy mean), apply channel weights, then convert with the same `-0.691 + 10*log10` mapping as ungated loudness.

A two-stage process is used. First an absolute threshold, then a relative threshold. Gating blocks below the absolute threshold are not used in the relative gating calculation. The relative threshold `Gamma_r` is calculated by measuring the loudness using the absolute threshold `Gamma_a = -70 LKFS` and subtracting 10 from the result:

```
Gamma_r = -0.691 + 10 * log10( sum_i G_i * (1/|J_g|) * sum_{j in J_g} z_ij ) - 10   LKFS
```

where `J_g = { j : l_j > Gamma_a }` and `Gamma_a = -70 LKFS`.                                                             (6)

The gated loudness is then calculated using `Gamma_r`:

```
Gated loudness, LK_G = -0.691 + 10 * log10( sum_i G_i * (1/|J_g|) * sum_{j in J_g} z_ij )   LKFS
```

where `J_g = { j : l_j > Gamma_r and l_j > Gamma_a }`.                                                                    (7)

Both thresholds are strict (`>`): a block whose loudness equals the threshold is not a survivor.

## Annex 2 — Guidelines for accurate measurement of true-peak level

This Annex describes an algorithm for estimation of true-peak level within a single-channel linear PCM digital audio signal. The discussion presumes a 48 kHz sample rate. True-peak level is the maximum (positive or negative) value of the signal waveform in the continuous time domain; this value may be higher than the largest sample value in the 48 kHz time-sampled domain.

### Summary of stages

1. Attenuate: 12.04 dB attenuation.
2. 4× over-sampling.
3. Low-pass filter.
4. Absolute: absolute value.
5. Conversion to dB TP.

The first step imposes an attenuation of 12.04 dB (2-bit shift). The purpose is to provide headroom for subsequent integer arithmetic. This step is not necessary if the calculations are performed in floating point.

The 4× over-sampling filter increases the sampling rate of the signal from 48 kHz to 192 kHz. This higher-sample-rate version of the signal more accurately indicates the actual waveform represented by the audio samples. Higher sampling rates and over-sampling ratios are preferred. Incoming signals that are at higher sampling rates require proportionately less over-sampling (for example, a 96 kHz incoming signal needs 2× over-sampling).

One set of filter coefficients (for the order-48, 4-phase FIR interpolating filter) that would satisfy the requirements is as follows. Each column is one polyphase; each row is one tap. Reading a column top-to-bottom is the 12-tap impulse response of that phase.

The absolute value of the samples is taken by inverting the negative-value samples; at this point the signal is unipolar, with negative values replaced by positive values of the same magnitude.

The result after the four stages (attenuation, oversampling, filtering, and taking the absolute value) is a number in the same domain as the original sample values. After this, it is necessary to compensate for the initial 12.04 dB attenuation. This normalises the overall gain of the processing to unity.

Amplification of the attenuated value by 12.04 dB will, in general, require conversion into a numeric format capable of representing values higher than the full-scale range of the original format. Performing the calculation steps in floating point satisfies this requirement. An alternative to amplifying the result is to calibrate the meter scale appropriately.

Meters that follow these guidelines, and that use an oversampled sampling rate of at least 192 kHz, should indicate the result in the units of dB TP, having converted the result to a logarithmic scale. This can be achieved by calculating `20 * log10` of the attenuated, oversampled, filtered, absolute value, then adding 12.04 dB. The designation "dB TP" signifies decibels relative to 100% full scale, true-peak measurement.

When the processing is performed in floating point without the 12.04 dB attenuation, the true-peak level of a channel is:

```
dBTP = 20 * log10( max(|interpolated samples|) )
```

over the oversampled, low-pass-filtered waveform, including any interpolator tail required to drain the FIR after the last input sample.

### Informative notes from Attachment 1 to Annex 2 (implementation context)

## Annex 3 and Annex 4 (out of scope for 3/2 and stereo)

Annex 3 extends Annex 1 to arbitrary loudspeaker layouts by making the channel weight `G_i` depend on azimuth and elevation (Table 4: `|phi| < 30°` and `60° ≤ |theta| ≤ 120°` receives 1.41; otherwise 1.00). Filtering and gating are otherwise identical to Annex 1. Annex 4 measures object-based programmes by rendering to a loudspeaker layout and then applying Annex 1 or 3. Neither annex changes K-weighting, gating geometry, or true-peak measurement.
